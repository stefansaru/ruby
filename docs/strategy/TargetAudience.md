# Target Audience

Researched 2026-11-24. Figures are quoted from the sources named; several come
from ad vendors, which is said where it applies.

## Who plays

- **Puzzle players in general** are about 74–75% women in the US and UK, and
  most are over 35 (Facebook Gaming / GameRefinery data via
  [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/puzzle-games-report)).
- **Sudoku players** are more balanced: about 54% women, average age about
  35.5, estimated from search behaviour
  ([Rascasse](https://rascasse.com/explore/us/sudoku-15582)).
- **The book's buyers** are adult puzzle-book and BookTok readers, with families
  reached through printable "family" sheets. The wave was strongest in Spain,
  the Netherlands and the English-speaking markets
  ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/01/murdoku-and-the-adult-activity-book-craze-the-trend-sweeping-spain),
  [JFK](https://jfk.nl/news/murdoku-puzzelboek-hype-2026-kopen-bestellen-reserveren/)).

## Why they play, and how

- **Motives:** stress relief (71% of US word and brain players) and passing
  time (Udonis).
- **Sessions:** a median of about 4 minutes, 7 for the top quarter; about 14
  minutes a day (GameAnalytics 2021 via Udonis). Number-puzzle players run
  longer sessions, about 20 minutes "every few days"
  ([Appodeal, Dec 2025](https://appodeal.com/blog/who-will-play-your-games-in-2026/)).
- **Tolerance:** "zero tolerance for bugs", and ads are accepted when the trade
  is "clear and honest" (Appodeal).

**What this means for us.** A case should be finishable in one short sitting,
and a longer case must survive being put down: progress in a case is saved
(APP-GAM-012).

## Ads

- About 80% of US puzzle players are open to in-app ads, and prefer one
  30-second ad per 10 minutes over frequent short ones; only 6–7% buy anything
  in a month (Udonis).
- **43% of puzzle gamers say they would quit if ads are too prevalent**
  ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/33-of-puzzle-gamers-view-ads-positively-but-43-will-quit-if-theyre-too-prevalent-how-can-game-makers-hit-the-sweet-spot/)).
- 64–79% say they are willing to watch rewarded ads (Tapjoy 2022, Google/Kantar
  2020). These are ad-vendor surveys and may flatter the format
  ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/mobile-gamers-now-prefer-ad-supported-games-over-pay-to-play-games-according-to-tapjoys-modern-mobile-gamer-2022-report)).

**What this means for us.** Rewarded video on the player's request is the
format this audience accepts; interruption is the one they leave over. See
[Monetisation.md](Monetisation.md).

## Personas

These are working sketches drawn from the data above, not interviewed people.

**The book solver.** Bought one of the puzzle books, finished it, wants more.
Knows the rules already and will notice at once if a clue is loose or a case
has two answers. Wants hard cases, good notes, and no hand-holding. *Serves:*
expert tier, per-suspect notes, labelled coordinates.

**The sudoku regular.** Plays a number puzzle most days, 35 or older, more
often a woman. New to the detective twist. Wants a gentle tutorial, a clear
difficulty ladder, and calm. Will watch an ad for a hint if asked politely;
will uninstall over an ad that barges in. *Serves:* tutorial, easy and normal
tiers, hints that explain, the ad rules.

**The curious newcomer.** Saw a clip of someone solving one. Has five minutes.
Needs to succeed in the first case without reading a rules page. *Serves:* the
4×4 tutorial, simple words, the clue preview.

**The family table.** A parent and a child solving together on a tablet.
Needs simple place names, a bloodless tone, and nothing alarming in an ad.
*Serves:* cosy tone, simple settings, content rating work (RiskAnalysis.md R4).
