# Target Audience

Researched 2026-11-24, extended 2026-11-25 with the findings on the book's
buyers and on what this audience pays for. Figures are quoted from the sources
named; several come from ad vendors or are weak proxies, which is said where it
applies. **There is no data on who plays this genre specifically**; everything
below is borrowed from neighbouring audiences.

## Who plays

- **Puzzle players in general** are about 74–75% women in the US and UK, and
  most are over 35 (Facebook Gaming / GameRefinery data via
  [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/puzzle-games-report)).
- **Sudoku players** are more balanced: about 54% women, average age about
  35, estimated from search behaviour
  ([Rascasse](https://rascasse.com/explore/us/sudoku-15582)).
- **A weak proxy from a web mahjong site's own analytics (2023–24):** 66%
  female, 50% aged 65 or over, 35% from the US and 7% from Germany
  ([themahjong.com](https://themahjong.com/blog/who-solves-puzzles-a-study-of-the-online-puzzle-game-audience)).
  One site, another puzzle; it shows only that calm web puzzles skew older and
  female.
- **The book's buyers** are adult puzzle-book and BookTok readers, with families
  reached through printable "family" sheets. *Murdoku* sold more than 140,000
  copies in Spain, driven by the streamer Cristinini and by TikTok, and reached
  number one on Amazon Spain; it is published in 15 languages; the Dutch stock
  sold out. No unit figures were found for Germany or the US
  ([Euronews, Sep 2026](https://www.euronews.com/culture/2026/09/01/murdoku-and-the-adult-activity-book-boom-a-phenomenon-sweeping-spain),
  [murdoku.com](https://murdoku.com/book),
  [JFK](https://jfk.nl/news/murdoku-puzzelboek-hype-2026-kopen-bestellen-reserveren/)).

**What this means for us.** The one audience we can count is in Spain, and it
arrived through video. Spanish is the first translation to consider
(Objectives.md H2), and Spain gets a lower regional price
([Monetisation.md](Monetisation.md)).

## Why they play, and how

- **Motives:** stress relief (71% of US word and brain players) and passing
  time (Udonis).
- **Sessions:** a median of about 4 minutes, 7 for the top quarter; about 14
  minutes a day (GameAnalytics 2021 via Udonis). Number-puzzle players run
  longer sessions, about 20 minutes "every few days"
  ([Appodeal, Dec 2025](https://appodeal.com/blog/who-will-play-your-games-in-2026/)).
- **Tolerance:** "zero tolerance for bugs" (Appodeal).

**What this means for us.** A case should be finishable in one short sitting,
and a longer case must survive being put down: progress in a case is saved
(APP-GAM-012).

## What this audience pays for

The game carries no ads (owner ruling 2026-11-25), so the question is what
these players buy. Figures as seen 2026-11-25; sources and the full tables are
in [MarketResearch.md](MarketResearch.md) §7.

- **They buy books.** The book that made the genre lists at $18.99 in the US,
  GBP 16.99 in the UK and about EUR 20 in the Netherlands for 80 puzzles: about
  $0.11–0.24 a puzzle depending on discount. This is the strongest evidence
  that the audience pays for puzzles at all.
- **They buy printables.** Logic-case PDFs on Etsy sell at $3.99 for 10 cases,
  EUR 10.33 for 45 and EUR 31.25 for 270.
- **In apps, they pay once.** Premium puzzle apps charge $3.99–4.99 for a
  one-time unlock or a pack (Good Sudoku, Cracking the Cryptic, Egghead's Logic
  Grid Puzzles and Star Battle). The genre's market leader sells ad removal at
  EUR 4.99; the one rival with no ads (Roomers) charges EUR 7.99 once.
- **Few buy at all.** Only 6–7% of puzzle players buy anything in a month
  (Udonis). No recent figure exists for how many players of a free puzzle buy a
  one-time unlock; old anchors are 0.67% to 3.7%, and the general free-to-play
  range is 2–5% (sources in [Monetisation.md](Monetisation.md)).
- **Ads are what they leave over.** 43% of puzzle gamers say they would quit if
  ads are too prevalent
  ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/33-of-puzzle-gamers-view-ads-positively-but-43-will-quit-if-theyre-too-prevalent-how-can-game-makers-hit-the-sweet-spot/)).
  Ad vendors' surveys report that 64–79% are willing to watch rewarded ads
  (Tapjoy 2022, Google/Kantar 2020); these may flatter the format, and they no
  longer bear on this game.
- **The market around them is not theirs.** Mobile puzzle in-app revenue was
  about $10B in 2025, dominated by match and merge games (Sensor Tower via
  [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/puzzle-games-rise-as-mobile-genres-compete-for-attention-in-2026/)).
  That number says little about a logic puzzle with one price.

**What this means for us.** A generous free part, no ads, and one purchase at
about the price of a quarter of the book. "No ads" is itself something this
audience values and a selling point. See [Monetisation.md](Monetisation.md).

## Personas

These are working sketches drawn from the data above, not interviewed people.

**The book solver.** Bought one of the puzzle books, finished it, wants more.
Knows the rules already and will notice at once if a clue is loose or a case
has two answers. Wants hard cases, good notes, and no hand-holding. Already
paid about $19 for 80 puzzles, so this is the likeliest buyer of the unlock:
the hard and expert packs are what they came for. *Serves:* expert tier,
per-suspect notes, labelled coordinates, the one purchase.

**The sudoku regular.** Plays a number puzzle most days, 35 or older, more
often a woman. New to the detective twist. Wants a gentle tutorial, a clear
difficulty ladder, and calm. Has uninstalled puzzle apps over ads that barge
in. May stay in the free packs for weeks, and buys when the easy pack runs out
and the price is plain. *Serves:* tutorial, easy and normal tiers, hints that
explain, no ads, the price shown before the gate.

**The curious newcomer.** Saw a clip of someone solving one. Has five minutes.
Needs to succeed in the first case without reading a rules page, and must not
meet a price before they have had fun. *Serves:* the 4×4 tutorial, simple
words, the clue preview, a free part with no timers.

**The family table.** A parent and a child solving together on a tablet.
Needs simple place names and a bloodless tone. With no ads in the game, nothing
from outside can appear on the screen. *Serves:* cosy tone, simple settings,
content rating work (RiskAnalysis.md R4).

**The cosy cat-puzzle buyer.** Likes the logic and avoids a murder theme, for
themselves or for children: the buyer of printables such as a cat-themed set of
50 "cosy cat logic mysteries" at $5.99 on Etsy (seen in a search snippet,
2026-11-25). A cat mode exists as a wording switch
([Modes.md](../features/Modes.md)). *Serves:* the cat mode, the bloodless
tone, no ads.
